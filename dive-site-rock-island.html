What you'll see at Rock Island

Hard corals in the shallows, with anemonefish, scorpionfish and lionfish. Cuttlefish and stingrays show up on most dives, so they get a mention in the briefing. The deeper you go, the fewer corals and the more sand.

Good to know

About ten minutes by boat. There can be current, and it is usually manageable. A mooring line runs from the boat to the reef, so you go down and come up along the line instead of drifting off. When the current is strong we don't turn it into a drift dive: we come back to the boat. Any time of day works. Once, between Rock Island and Semibayan, we met a dugong.

When we can't go

Almost never. Only a strong typhoon or very strong wind keeps us off Rock Island. See the diving season.

Who it suits

All levels, from Discover Scuba and courses to rescue divers, divemasters and instructors. Together with Semibayan, it's where we take most of our divers.

Dive Rock Island with us

A fun dive is two dives for ₱5,000 per person (private ₱8,500), with all gear, the boat and the marine fee included. You need an Open Water certification, and groups are five divers at most. Never dived? Start with Discover Scuba. Friends who don't dive can come along to snorkel. We choose the site for your level and the day, so ask us on WhatsApp what is possible on your dates.
